# Find Me Cuddle — Marketing Plan

## Snapshot

Find Me Cuddle is a two-sided marketplace: cuddlers (supply) and clients (demand). Marketplace theory is consistent on sequencing — fix supply first, in one metro at a time, before spending real effort on demand-side marketing. A directory with five cuddlers in five different states converts worse than a directory with fifteen cuddlers in one city. Recommendation: pick one or two beachhead metros (wherever the current cuddler concentration is strongest) and get disproportionately loud there before going national.

The closest direct comparable, Cuddlist, runs organic content on YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok, but their heavier lift is earned media — regular print, TV, radio, and podcast features — plus training their own practitioners to market themselves. That mix (light paid, heavy organic + PR + practitioner-driven content) is the right model here too, for a reason that's specific to this category: paid ads are genuinely risky.

## The platform-risk issue, first

Both TikTok's and Meta's (Instagram/Facebook) ad policies prohibit paid promotion of "companionship or relationships in exchange for compensation," and their automated ad review can't reliably tell "professional platonic touch therapy" apart from other paid-companionship categories. This mainly affects **paid ads** — running Instagram/TikTok ad campaigns for Find Me Cuddle risks account suspension, not just a rejected ad. Organic (unpaid) posts are reviewed less aggressively but aren't risk-free either — automated moderation still keys off phrases like "book a session," "pay for cuddling," or similar, so wording matters even in regular posts.

Practical implication for the plan below: lead with organic content, wellness/therapy framing, and earned press, and treat paid social ads as a "maybe later, budget permitting, start small and monitor" line rather than a core channel. Google Search/Local ads don't carry this same restriction and are a safer place to put paid budget first.

## Platform-by-platform plan

### Instagram — the hub

Your instinct to post each new cuddler is the right foundation. Structure it as a repeatable content series rather than one-off posts:

- **"Meet Your Cuddler" series** — a consistent template (photo, name, city, one or two "getting to know me" facts already on their profile, a line about what drew them to this work) posted every time someone new goes live. Consistency here matters more than production value; it becomes a recognizable, followable format.
- **City spotlights** — once a metro has 5+ cuddlers, a "Cuddlers Now Available In [City]" carousel roundup drives local relevance and gives you reusable content that doesn't require anything new from anyone.
- **Educational/destigmatizing content** — short posts or Reels on touch deprivation, what a session actually looks like, consent and boundaries, "is this therapy" FAQs. This is the content that gets shared and builds trust with a skeptical audience, and it's also the safest content from a platform-policy standpoint since it's not transactional.
- **Stories for urgency/freshness** — "Available Now" activations, last-minute openings, new-to-the-platform announcements.

Avoid direct "book now" / pricing language in captions; link out to the profile instead and let the site do the converting.

### TikTok / Instagram Reels — yes to cuddler-made content, with a program around it

This is a strong idea and matches what Cuddlist already does successfully, but it should be a structured, opt-in program rather than an ask-anyone-anytime thing, for two reasons: consistency (some cuddlers will be great on camera, some won't want to be, and that's fine) and liability (make sure what gets posted reflects the brand you want and doesn't create TOS/consent issues).

**"Cuddler Voices" ambassador program** — outline:
- Opt-in, not mandatory. Frame it as a growth opportunity ("cuddlers who post tend to get more inquiries") rather than a requirement.
- Give them 5–10 content prompts/hooks to remove the blank-page problem: "Why I became a professional cuddler," "What people get wrong about this job," "A day in the life," "The most common thing clients tell me," "Boundaries 101 — what a session actually involves." Destigmatizing/educational angles perform better and are safer than anything transactional-sounding.
- Simple content guidelines doc: no explicit/suggestive content, no specific-client details (privacy), always frame as platonic/therapeutic, tag @findmecuddle and their own profile link, avoid the word "book" in on-camera hooks (say "reach out" or "find me on the site" instead).
- Consider a small incentive — a featured spot on the homepage or Instagram, a discount on their next month's listing fee, or a small referral-style bonus for content that visibly drives signups (trackable via a UTM link or promo code).
- Cross-post their best content to the main @findmecuddle account (with credit) — this is free, authentic content that's more persuasive than anything you'd produce centrally.

### Beyond Instagram/TikTok — other channels worth the effort

- **Google Business Profile — individual cuddlers, not the platform.** Find Me Cuddle itself doesn't qualify: Google explicitly excludes lead-gen/directory businesses with no in-person customer contact of their own. Individual cuddlers do qualify, though, as "service-area businesses" — the same category mobile massage therapists and plumbers use. No public address is shown (a real, non-PO-box address is required for verification, but it stays hidden), and the service radius is capped around a 2-hour drive. Worth adding a short how-to guide to the Cuddler Voices program: each cuddler sets up their own profile (closest available category, e.g. "Massage therapist" or "Wellness center," since cuddle therapy isn't a standard option), linking back to their Find Me Cuddle listing. This gets the local-search benefit without the platform running afoul of Google's eligibility rules.
- **Local SEO on the site itself** — city landing pages for each beachhead metro (worth revisiting the "SEO city landing pages" note already in the codebase) is the platform-level equivalent, and something you control directly instead of relying on individual cuddlers to set up their own profiles.
- **Reddit** — city-specific subreddits (r/[city]) and wellness-adjacent ones (r/selfcare, r/touchdeprivation-type communities, r/AsianTherapy-style niche wellness subs where relevant) respond well to genuine, non-salesy posts — "AMA" style posts from a cuddler, or a founder post explaining what the platform is and why it exists, tend to outperform anything that reads like an ad. Reddit's culture punishes obvious marketing hard, so this needs a real person, not a brand account, and shouldn't be attempted until you're ready to genuinely engage in the comments.
- **Local press / human-interest media** — this is the single highest-leverage channel for a category like this, per Cuddlist's own strategy. Local news, city-specific blogs/newsletters, and even national outlets (Newsweek, Vice, and similar have all covered this niche before) regularly run human-interest pieces on professional cuddling. A short pitch email to 5–10 local journalists/podcasters per beachhead city, offering an interview with a cuddler plus you as the platform founder, costs nothing but time and can outperform months of paid ads in credibility and reach.
- **YouTube Shorts** — same content as TikTok/Reels can be repurposed here with near-zero extra effort; YouTube's discovery algorithm for Shorts is also generally friendlier to wellness/educational content.
- **Pinterest** — smaller lift, but "self-care," "stress relief," and "touch therapy" are active Pinterest search categories, and pins have a long shelf life compared to social posts. Good place to repost the "Meet Your Cuddler" and educational graphics.
- **Facebook Groups** — local wellness, self-care, singles/social-connection, and caregiver-support groups (with admin permission, following each group's self-promo rules) are worth testing; Facebook's community feels older/more local than Instagram's and may overlap well with your actual client base.
- **Partnerships** — therapists, massage/wellness studios, yoga studios, and grief/anxiety support groups are natural referral partners; a simple one-pager or referral arrangement (even informal) can be worth more than a month of ad spend.

## Suggested cadence (once running)

- Instagram: 3–5 posts/week (mostly "Meet Your Cuddler" + educational), daily Stories when there's anything timely (new cuddler, Available Now activation).
- Reels/TikTok/Shorts: 2–3/week to start, sourced primarily from the Cuddler Voices program plus 1 centrally-produced educational piece.
- Reddit/press outreach: ongoing, low-frequency, high-effort-per-post.
- Google Business Profile: each opted-in cuddler sets up and maintains their own; monthly posts/updates help (Google rewards recency).

## 90-day starting plan

1. **Weeks 1–2:** Write the Cuddler Voices program one-pager and content-prompt list, including the Google Business Profile how-to; invite current cuddlers to opt in.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** Launch the "Meet Your Cuddler" Instagram series (retroactively cover existing cuddlers, then keep pace with new signups). Start weekly educational content. Draft and send press pitches to 5–10 local outlets/podcasts in your beachhead city.
3. **Weeks 7–10:** First cuddler-made Reels/TikToks go live, cross-posted to the main account. Test 2–3 relevant subreddits with genuine, non-promotional posts. Reach out to 3–5 potential wellness-adjacent partners.
4. **Weeks 11–13:** Review what actually drove signups/inquiries (track via UTM links or a simple "how did you hear about us" field on signup); double down on whichever 2–3 channels performed, cut or deprioritize the rest.

## What to measure

- New cuddler signups per week/metro (supply-side health — the marketplace theory point above).
- Inquiries ("Send My Info" submissions) per week, and which channel/link they came from (UTM tagging is cheap and worth setting up now).
- Cuddler participation rate in the content program (a good proxy for whether the incentive structure is working).
- Instagram/TikTok follower growth is a vanity metric here — track it, but don't optimize for it directly; optimize for signups and inquiries.

---

*A note on the reels idea specifically, since you asked directly: yes, cuddlers making their own short-form content is very likely worth doing — it's authentic, it's free, and it's what your closest comparable already leans on. The two things that make it work well instead of backfiring are (1) making it genuinely opt-in with easy prompts so it doesn't feel like an obligation, and (2) keeping the framing therapeutic/educational rather than transactional, both for platform-policy safety and because that framing is simply what performs better with a skeptical audience on this topic.*
